# Salvage King's Claw — detail-opus5 self-report

`salvage-claw-detail-opus5.glb` · 30,100 tris (ceiling 45,000) · 1 material · 1 embedded 2048² atlas · 3 draw calls · byte-identical re-export under Blender 5.1.2 · base SHA `0edb3d7e`.

1. **I prioritised the three things the plate has and the shipped model does not**: a spire *skyline* instead of four cones, a visible under-rim machinery band (paired ribbed drums, hanging plaques, suspension cables), and anchor feet that read as grapples rather than flippers. Those three are most of the fidelity gap.
2. **A `minaret` helper made the skyline affordable** — shaft, banded bulb, spike, teal orb — so eighteen perimeter turrets, eight inner spires and four corner spires cost one function. Repetition is where a raised ceiling actually pays: the shipped model could not afford the *repeat*, not the shape. Density is the read; a handful of fatter cones is not a cathedral horizon.
3. **The feet are swept tubes, three talons each, curving out and hooking down.** Same helper I cut for the Queen's grab. Twelve curved talons for ~3k triangles is far better value than the shipped icosphere-plus-flipper, and it makes the settle morph legible because there is a shape to buckle.
4. **The four legs sit at 45°, so the crown — not the feet — sets the X extent the normaliser scales by.** My first build exported 12.95 units tall against the shipped 10.031, a 29% overshoot that would have towered in-scene. I diagnosed it from the bounds, retuned the vertical stack, and landed 9.965. This was the one real trap on this boss and I would check it first next time.
5. **Two elements failed their first render and I caught both by looking.** The claim pendants were tucked inside the rim silhouette and vanished; the winch drums were swallowed by the belly cone. I moved the pendants outboard and below, and dropped the drums under the belly skirt into open air, which is where the plate stages both.
6. **Canon held, including the quiet clauses.** Pendant marks are pictograms only — anchor, crossed tools, claw stamp, ledger rule — never letters, per the roster brief. No people in the production model, per the contract's state law. The landed state stays a future civic yard: shutters close, ladders unroll, nothing burns.
7. **Every clause of the runtime guard is preserved** — three meshes named `winch`/`anchor_feet`/`crown`, one `Landing_*` morph per mesh at index 0 with default weight 0, identity transforms, one material, zero cameras/lights/animations, 11.4 footprint, base on z=0. Adoption is one integer at `SalvageClawBossSystem.ts:16` (`10_164 → 30_100`). I did not edit `src/`.
8. **Measured cost:** draw calls unchanged at 3; geometry 0.74→2.17 MB; morph storage 0.29→0.86 MB; texture 5.33→21.33 MB. As with the Queen, **the 2048 atlas is 16 MB of the 17.4 MB delta** — if the owner wants the detail without the memory, drop to 1024 and keep every triangle.
9. **What I would do with more time, first:** the anchor feet are the one element I could not grow. The plate's talons are enormous crescents that dominate the read; mine are correct in shape but modest in scale, and enlarging them pushes the outer toes past the promenade radius, which hands the X extent to the feet and rescales the whole body. Fixing it properly means re-deriving the vertical stack against a feet-driven extent — worth doing, too risky to do last.
10. **What I would do with more time, second:** the drums still under-read from a top-down gameplay camera even after I dropped them below the belly skirt, and I have 15k triangles (33%) still unspent. I would spend them on promenade furniture and the central pendant — but only after running the blind play-camera check, because everything I fixed on this boss, I found by looking at a render rather than by reasoning about the geometry.
